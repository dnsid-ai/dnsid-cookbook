## Analysis

- **Public share of spending:** Japan (85%), the UK (83%) and Germany (78%) pay for most care publicly. The US (57%) and Brazil (45%) pay for much less of it publicly.
- **The US is an outlier on total spending:** It spends 17.4% of GDP and $12,000 per person. That's about 1.6× Germany and more than 2× the UK or Japan. Its lower public share is mostly made up by private insurance, not by patients paying directly. Its out-of-pocket share (10%) is actually the lowest of the five.
- **Brazil's patients pay the most directly:** Brazil has the lowest public share and the highest out-of-pocket share (23%), about twice the level of the other four. It also spends far less per person ($1,600). Even so, 9.9% of GDP is a lot for an upper-middle-income country.
- **The UK and Japan are similar:** Both spend about $5,300 per person, mostly from public money, with out-of-pocket shares around 12–13%. Japan gets there with a smaller share of GDP (10.8% vs 12.4%).
- **Germany is in between:** It spends more than the UK and Japan, both per person and as a share of GDP, with a slightly lower public share.
